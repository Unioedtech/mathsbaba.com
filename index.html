Why Maths Baba

Twenty years,
one way of teaching.

There's a reason parents send the younger sibling after the older one. Here is what they tell us.

01

Small batches, by class and board

A CBSE batch and an ICSE batch are different batches. Applied Maths is its own batch. Nobody sits through a syllabus that is not theirs.

02

A test every week

Chapter tests through the term, then full board-pattern papers from the second term of Class 10 and Class 12. Writing under time is practised, not hoped for.

03

Every answer sheet checked the same day

Step marks, the wrong line underlined, a note in the margin. On your phone the same evening, before the mistake is forgotten.

Q4 · 3/5 · cofactorQ7 · 4/5 · sign
04

Every student gets unique practice and homework

Every slip is filed by topic. The next practice set is six questions on exactly those topics, not the whole chapter again. By the board exam, each student knows their own list.

05

A report for parents every fortnight

Not just the marks. The patterns behind them: where marks leak, which mistakes keep repeating, and whether last fortnight's slips have stopped. The PTM starts from the pattern, not from memory.

Classes and batches

Class 6 to Class 12. Two programmes.

Each batch sits with its own class and board. Timings for your batch on the call.

Foundation6 – 10
  • CBSE and ICSE batches run separately, on their own textbooks and marking schemes
  • Weekly chapter tests, so the habit of reading a checked answer sheet starts early
  • Class 10 board batch writes full-length papers from the second term
